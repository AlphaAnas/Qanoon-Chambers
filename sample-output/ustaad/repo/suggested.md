S2 | Khan v. Ali — recovery suit on dishonoured cheque
- Source: Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, Order XXXVII, rules 1–3 (official)
- Heading: “SUMMARY PROCEDURE ON NEGOTIABLE INSTRUMENTS”
- Summary: Rule 2 provides a summary suit procedure on bills of exchange, hundies and promissory notes; Rule 3 governs leave to defend. Rule 1 limits which courts may use this procedure: High Court, District Court, and other Civil Courts notified by the High Court. A cheque is defined as a bill of exchange by section 6 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881.
- Why it matters: This is the key procedural route to check for the recovery claim, but confirm the competent/designated court for Karachi South and that the pleadings/instrument qualify before relying on Order XXXVII.

S3 | Khan v. Ali — recovery suit on dishonoured cheque
- Source: Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, sections 30 and 118 (official)
- Heading: “Liability of drawer” / “Presumptions as to negotiable instruments”
- Summary: Section 30 states the drawer of a dishonoured cheque engages to compensate its holder, provided due notice of dishonour has been given to or received by the drawer. Section 118(a) presumes consideration until the contrary is proved.
- Why it matters: These provisions identify the asserted instrument liability and statutory presumption; the cheque, proof of dishonour/notice, transaction documents and any rebuttal remain matters for the court on the record.
- URL: https://pakistancode.gov.pk/pdffiles/administrator2bbb145adb573172ec68151f4e70dfb5.pdf

S4 | Khan v. Ali — recovery suit on dishonoured cheque
- Source: Raja Parmanand v. Shamsher, Ist Appeal No. 113 of 2023, Sindh High Court, Karachi, decided 11 May 2026, paras. 1–2, 5–7 (official)
- Heading: “although Section 118 of the Negotiable Instruments Act gives rise to a presumption that a cheque has been issued for consideration, such presumption is not absolute in nature”
- Summary: In a Karachi South summary cheque suit where an FIR under section 489-F PPC had also been lodged, the High Court treated the section 118 presumption as rebuttable and examined evidence of the underlying debt; it upheld dismissal where the debt/consideration was not satisfactorily established.
- Why it matters: This is a recent local appellate example involving the same court district and concurrent 489-F context; its result turned on that record, so it is an evidentiary example rather than a prediction for this case.
- URL: https://caselaw.shc.gov.pk/caselaw/view-file/MzE0MTc5Y2Ztcy1kYzgz

S5 | Khan v. Ali — Order XXXVII forum/designation
- Source: Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, Order XXXVII rules 1–3 (official primary legislation); Muhammad Ahmed Siddiqui & another v Abdul Abid Advocate & another, I.A. Nos. 76 & 77 of 2018, Sindh High Court, order dated 30 July 2020, para. 12 (official judgment)
- Heading: “Application of Order”; “No notification has been issued by this High Court”
- Summary: Rule 1 authorizes Order XXXVII proceedings in the High Court, District Court, and another Civil Court notified by the High Court. The SHC said in 2020 no such notification had then conferred that power on a civil court in Sindh, and confined the summary jurisdiction to the High Court and District Courts. This is a date-specific finding; no later notification was located in the official materials checked, so counsel should verify current SHC notifications and the precise court designation. An ordinary civil suit is a separate route.
- Why it matters: “Civil Court Karachi South” does not identify whether this is a District Court or a Civil Judge; for a Civil Judge, Order XXXVII should not be assumed without applicable designation/notification. The case involves a cheque, which the NI Act defines as a bill of exchange, but forum eligibility remains distinct.

S6 | Khan v. Ali — cheque liability, notice, and interest/markup
- Source: Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, sections 30, 72, 79–80, 92–94, 98, 105–106 (official primary legislation); Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, section 34 and Order XXXVII rule 2(2) (official primary legislation)
- Heading: “Liability of drawer”; “Presentment of cheque to charge drawer”; “Interest when no rate specified”; “Mode in which notice may be given”; “Interest”
- Summary: NI Act s30 conditions drawer compensation on dishonour and due notice; s72 addresses presentment; ss93–94 set out who must receive notice, its content/mode and reasonable time; s98 lists exceptions. NI Act ss79–80 govern interest on the instrument (including specified instrument rate or statutory default); CPC O37 r2(2) refers to those provisions before suit and CPC s34 to court-awarded interest from suit onward. Plead the actual source, rate and period claimed and substantiate any separate loan markup agreement; identify and produce the cheque, bank return memo, presentation details, notice and proof of dispatch/receipt, or facts supporting a specific s98 exception. The present facts leave the agreed markup rate and notice chronology unconfirmed.
- Why it matters: The requested markup from 13 Sep 2026 and notice/presentment basis need case-specific factual support; these provisions do not establish an unpleaded contract rate by themselves.

S7 | Khan v. Ali — territorial jurisdiction and valuation
- Source: Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, sections 15, 20 and Order VII rules 1(e)–(i), 2 (official primary legislation); Sindh Civil Courts (Amendment) Act, 2025, Sindh Act VI of 2025, section 2 (official primary legislation); District & Sessions Courts Karachi Central, “Pecuniary Jurisdiction” (official court-administration information)
- Heading: “Other suits to be instituted where defendants reside or cause of action arises”; “Particulars to be contained in plaint”; “Original jurisdiction of the Court of District Judge”
- Summary: CPC s20 permits venue where defendant resides/carries on business/works for gain or where cause of action wholly/partly arises; O7 r1(f),(i) requires pleaded jurisdiction facts and valuation for jurisdiction/court fees, and r2 requires a precise money claim; s15 directs institution in the lowest-grade competent court. Sindh Act VI of 2025 substituted s7 to give District Judges original civil jurisdiction without value limit; the Karachi Central courts’ admin page reports Senior Civil Judges unlimited and Civil Judges up to Rs15m following an Administrator Committee revision under s9.
- Why it matters: The stated principal Rs1.5m is below the published Civil Judge pecuniary cap for ordinary civil suits, but this does not confer Order XXXVII jurisdiction. The defendant address supplied is Gulshan-e-Iqbal while suit is proposed in Karachi South; counsel should confirm and plead the actual South-district residence/business or cause-of-action facts and confirm valuation/court-fee treatment, including any interest sought.
